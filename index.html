How to read this
  • Gusts are wind from higher up, carried down by eddies. The stirred (mixed) layer is how high they reach; its lid is usually an inversion. Its strongest wind is the gust ceiling.
  • Sea − air (🌊 in Layers): + sea warmer, the air heated from below: stirred, gusty, showers. − air warmer: stable, the surface cut off from the wind aloft, smoother, fog-prone.
  • VAD vs airports: a lot of wind aloft but light at the surface means stable or sheltered; similar speeds or big gusts mean it's mixing down. Surface windier than the VAD: local funnelling the radar's average can't see.
  • Gust factor (gust ÷ mean): ~1.3 ordinary gusty mixing; 1.5+ strong mixing or squalls.
  • Rule of thumb: unstable + deep mixed layer + strong wind aloft: gusts toward the VAD speed, reef early. Stable + shallow: lighter wind, but it can arrive suddenly off headlands and in passes.
  • All observed: the balloon can be 12 h old and far away; the VAD is a ring round the radar; tide stations are harbour gauges. 📖 The full explanation
